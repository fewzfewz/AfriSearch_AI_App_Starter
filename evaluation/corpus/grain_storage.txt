Safe Grain Storage for Smallholder Farmers

This guide is an illustrative sample written for testing AfriSearch. It covers how to store maize and other grains after harvest to reduce losses.

Drying. Grain must be dried well before storage. Maize should be dried to a moisture content of 13 percent or less; above that level, moulds grow and can produce aflatoxins, which are poisonous to people and livestock. A simple test is the salt test: put a handful of grain and two spoonfuls of dry salt in a dry glass jar, shake it for three minutes and let it stand for fifteen minutes. If salt sticks to the sides of the jar, the grain is still too wet to store.

Drying surfaces. Dry grain on tarpaulins, mats or raised platforms, never directly on bare soil, which adds moisture and dirt. Cover the grain at night and when rain threatens.

Hermetic storage. Hermetic (airtight) bags and metal silos are the most effective way to protect grain from insects without chemicals. Once a bag is sealed, the insects inside use up the oxygen and die within a few weeks. Each hermetic bag usually has two inner plastic liners inside an outer woven bag. Squeeze out as much air as possible and tie each liner separately. Check the bags regularly for holes made by rodents, and patch them with tape.

Storage place. Store bags on pallets or wooden planks at least 10 centimetres above the floor and away from walls, so air can circulate and pests can be seen. Keep the store clean, dry and free of old grain from previous seasons.

Losses. Without good drying and storage, smallholder farmers can lose 20 to 30 percent of their harvest to insects, moulds and rodents.
