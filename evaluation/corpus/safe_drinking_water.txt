Making Drinking Water Safe at Home

This guide is an illustrative sample written for testing AfriSearch. It summarises common household methods for treating drinking water.

Boiling. Boiling is the most reliable way to kill germs. Bring the water to a rolling boil for at least one minute. At altitudes above 2,000 metres, such as in parts of the Ethiopian highlands, boil the water for three minutes, because water boils at a lower temperature there. Let the water cool in the same covered pot.

Chlorine. If boiling is not possible, use household chlorine products sold for water treatment and follow the dose on the label. After adding chlorine, wait at least 30 minutes before drinking. If the water is cloudy, filter it through a clean cloth first and use a double dose.

Solar disinfection (SODIS). Fill clear plastic PET bottles of up to two litres with clear water and leave them in full sunlight for at least six hours. If the sky is more than half cloudy, leave them for two consecutive days. SODIS does not work with cloudy water or glass bottles.

Filters. Ceramic and sand filters remove many germs and particles but should be cleaned regularly as the manufacturer instructs. Water from filters can still be boiled or chlorinated for extra safety.

Storage. Store treated water in a clean container with a narrow opening and a lid, and pour it out rather than dipping cups into it. Clean the container every week.
